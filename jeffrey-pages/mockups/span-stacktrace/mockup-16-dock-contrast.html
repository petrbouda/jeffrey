The docked strip: smaller, and told apart

Option 1 is settled — Select … is btn-outline-primary in the toolbar, and it is drawn that way in every panel here. What varies is the strip itself: each one is the same size reduction with a different answer to “tell it apart from the card”. Drawn inside the real card, with the toolbar above and bars below, because contrast against nothing is not contrast. The chips are measured by the page as it loads.

0 Todaythe problem

.exc-dock is --color-bg-card and so is .wf-card under it — the strip body and the card are the same white, told apart by a 1px --color-border-input line and a --shadow-sm you have to look for. The chips say it: body same, and only the header carries a faint 1.04:1 tint. Every panel below also applies the size reduction, so this one is the only one at today’s padding.

1 Sunkenrecommended

The strip drops to --color-lighter (#edf2f9) with an inset shadow, so it reads as a well cut into the card rather than a card lying on a card — which is what it physically is: the bars part to make room for it. The fold bars and the JVM line flip to white so they stay visible against the new ground. --color-light (#f9fafd) is the softer sibling if this is a step too far — it measures 1.04:1 where this measures 1.12:1.

3 Severity wash

The strip takes a wash of the colour of the throw it belongs to — --color-danger-bg-lighter here, and the escaped case would be stronger. Ties the panel to the mark on the rail that opened it, which nothing currently does. The cost is that a red-tinted panel says “error” about the whole strip, when most of what is in it is ordinary library frames.

4 Banded header

Body stays white; the header becomes a solid --color-danger band with the label and the close in white. Also the loudest thing in the dialog by some margin, for a panel the reader opened deliberately.

The pick, and what "smaller" actually costs

1, sunken. The strip pushes the bars apart to make room for itself, so a well cut into the card is what it physically is — and --color-lighter against --color-bg-card is a separation the app already uses everywhere else, not a new idea. 2 is the one to avoid if the ask is contrast: its grounds stay identical, and the chip above it says so.

3, 4 and 5 all spend the danger colour on a panel whose content is mostly ordinary library frames. They are the right answer only if you want the strip to keep announcing which rail mark opened it — which is a real thing nothing currently does, just not the thing that was asked for.

The numbers are small, and that is worth knowing before reading them. A light ground against white is a perceptual separation, not a high-ratio one: the sunken variant measures 1.12:1 and the softer --color-light only 1.04:1, while the solid band in 4 is the only option here that produces a large number (4.16:1). So the chips rank the options honestly but they do not say "1.12 is barely anything" — at these values the border and the white fold bars inside do as much separating as the fill. If what you want is a step change rather than a seam, 4 is the only one of the five that delivers it.

The size reduction is the same in all five, so the chips compare like with like: header padding 0.4/0.6rem to 0.26/0.5rem, stack padding 0.35/0.6/0.5 to 0.25/0.5/0.35, the close button 1.4rem to 1.2rem, the frame rows a hair tighter, and the scroll cap 20rem to 17rem. The cap is the only one with a real cost: a long stack scrolls sooner. Everything else is padding the strip was not using.
