The pick, and what X actually costs

2 — inset 3rem, cap 24rem. The inset is what makes Option 2 work: shadow on four sides instead of two, so the strip reads as raised over the bars rather than as a band cut into them. The cap goes from 17rem to 24rem, about 40% more stack per screenful, which is the dimension that was actually short — the content is a 253-frame list.

I expected X to be bounded by the frames. Measured, it is not. A frame row is grid-template-columns: 1fr auto with an ellipsised signature, so I assumed the gutter would start eating frame names — the chips count them, and all five clip zero of 33 at any realistic width. Sweeping the viewport from 1920px down, the first clip appears at a strip width near 700px, which none of these reach until the browser window is under about 950px. So the inset is free, and the choice between them is a judgement about how much white belongs inside the panel — not a trade against the content.

Y is bounded by the waterfall. The strip pushes the bars down rather than covering them, so height it takes is height they lose. 5 is there to show where that stops being a good trade — 30rem is most of a laptop viewport, and the panel starts crowding out the thing it belongs to.
