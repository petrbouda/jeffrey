Not a popover. If you want one answer

3, at 34×22. It is the only one that fixes the truncation rather than deferring it, and fixing that is what makes the extra width pay: with the packages no longer spending the row, 34rem says more than 38rem does in direction 2. Nine frames is the number that matters here — it is what reaches past the OGNL parser to the application frame that called it.

1 is the change to make if the truncation does not bother you. 4 and 5 are bigger rewrites that each trade something the current design was deliberate about: 4 inverts on a long message, and 5 moves the bars under the reader's cursor.

Noticed while drawing this — not a size problem

The throwing frame on your screenshot is java.lang.Throwable.<init>, drawn in red as the line that threw. It is not: jdk.JavaExceptionThrow samples the stack in the Throwable constructor, so the top two or three frames of every exception are always the constructor chain — Throwable.<init>, Exception.<init>, IOException.<init>. The frame that actually threw is the first one below them.

So the red is on the wrong row, and one of the nine is spent saying nothing. Worth folding the constructor chain away and moving the throwing-frame mark to the first frame beneath it — a separate change from this one, and probably worth more.
