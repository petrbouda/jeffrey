5The stack, minus the noise

The direction that attacks the actual problem rather than where to put it. Of these 45 frames, eight are the reader's own code; the rest are Tomcat, Spring, reflection and the streams runtime carrying it. So runs of consecutive library frames collapse into one expandable bar — ▸ 15 frames in org.apache.catalina — and what stays visible is the throwing frame, the application frames, and the shape of what sits between them. What it buys

9 rows instead of 45, with nothing lost. Every folded frame is one click away, so this is not truncation — it is ordering by what the reader came for. It also makes direction 1 viable: at this height a stack fits inline without pushing the panel apart. The classification already exists in the parser — StacktraceTypeResolver and LambdaMatchUtils already decide what counts as application code.

What it costs

It has an opinion, and opinions are wrong sometimes. A reader debugging inside Spring wants the frames this hides, and the fold makes them work for it. The rule also has to be right about what "the application" is — a stack that is all library frames folds to almost nothing, which reads as a bug rather than as an answer, so that case needs handling explicitly.
