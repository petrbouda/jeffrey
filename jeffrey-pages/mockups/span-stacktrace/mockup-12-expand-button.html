Expand button, under the time offset

Option 5’s idea — one affordance per row, in the gutter — with the button moved off the caret line and stacked under the offset. The row stays clickable; the button is what says so. Five skins for that button, all in the same place. In each panel the first row is drawn hovered, the second has no stack at all so the gutter shows what a row without a control looks like, the third is a throw with no message — the short row, and the only one the stacked gutter makes taller — and the last is open.

1 Ghostrecommended

No frame at rest, so the gutter at rest is what it always was — a column of timestamps. The button draws itself when the row lights up and stays filled while its stack is open, which is the same rest/hover/on progression the ghost variant had at the right edge, only now it costs no width from the message column. The quietest of the five, and the one whose resting list is closest to today’s.

What moving it under the offset actually bought

The objection to Option 5 was its position, not its idea. One affordance per row is the right shape; putting it at the left edge inline with the offset was what pinned it into a 1rem column and left the right side of the row as empty as before. Stacking it under the offset keeps the single affordance and drops the 1rem caret column entirely — the grid goes from 1rem 4.5rem 1fr to 4.5rem 1fr.

Measured, not assumed. On a throw with a message the row is 51px tall and the stacked gutter comes to 41px, so the button sits entirely in space the layout was already throwing away — zero added height. On a throw with no message the text is only 33px, and the gutter’s 41px becomes the taller of the two: that row grows by 8px. It is the third row in every panel above, drawn short on purpose. So the move is free on the common row and costs eight pixels on the short one — which is the whole bill, and it is the same bill for all five skins, since every button here is 24px tall.

1 is the recommendation: nothing is added to the resting list, and the control appears on the row you are pointing at. 3 if you would rather every row that has a stack say so without being pointed at. 4 is the most useful of the five and the only one that is not purely a frontend change.
