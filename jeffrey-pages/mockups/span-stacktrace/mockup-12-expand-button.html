Expand button for a throw

The row stays clickable — the button is an additional affordance, not a replacement, so a reader can hit either. All five put it at the far right, which is the point: that empty stretch was what made the hover look like a slab, and a control there gives the width a reason to exist. In each panel the first row is hovered and the last is open, so both states are visible. Hover is the same quiet --color-bg-hover everywhere; only the button varies.

1 Ghost icon buttonrecommended

A round icon button at the row’s end: invisible frame at rest, gaining a border and the primary tint when the row lights up. No label, because the region header already says these are exceptions and the chevron says the rest. The quietest thing that is still unmistakably a button — and at the right edge it reads as "this row opens" rather than as decoration.

3 The count is the labelneeds backend

The same button, carrying a fact instead of a word: 18 frames. The reader learns how deep the stack is before deciding to open it, and a one-frame stack stops being worth a click. It needs a backend change: a row knows its stacktraceId but not its frame count, so this means adding the count to the trace_exceptions derivation — cheap, but not free, and not something the frontend can do alone.

4 Nothing until you point at it

Absent at rest, a solid primary button on hover, and stays solid while its stack is open. The list at rest is nothing but the throws — the cleanest reading of the four rows — and the control appears exactly when it can be used. The cost is real: a control you cannot see is a control a reader may never learn is there, and it does nothing at all for touch.

5 The gutter caret becomes the button

No second control: the chevron already at the left edge grows a frame and becomes the button. The row keeps exactly one affordance, and it sits where a tree or an accordion would put it. But it puts the target back at the narrow end and leaves the right side as empty as it was — which is the thing that started this.

If you want one answer

1. It is a button, so the row stops relying on a reader guessing it is clickable, and it goes in the space that was making the hover look wrong — the fill now has something at its far end instead of running out into nothing. Quiet enough that four of them down a list do not become the loudest thing in the panel.

3 is the one to want if you are willing to spend a backend change on it. Knowing a stack is 45 frames versus 3 before clicking is worth more than any styling on this page, and the count is already sitting in the derivation one column away.

4 looks the best in a screenshot and is the riskiest in use — hover-only controls are invisible to anyone who does not already know they exist. 5 is the tidiest markup and does nothing about the width.
