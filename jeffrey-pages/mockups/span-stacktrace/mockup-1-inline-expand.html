1Disclosure in place

The throw row grows a caret. Clicking it unfolds the frames underneath, inside the Exceptions region the reader is already looking at — nothing new opens, nothing moves, and the panel stays a single scroll. Two throws here stay collapsed and one is open, which is the state a reader actually lands in: the escaped one is the one you expand. The stack keeps the caught throws' rows one line each, so the list still reads as a list.

What it buys

Cheapest to build and the least disruptive. It is one v-if and one fetch inside a component that already renders the row. Nothing about the modal's layout changes, so it cannot break the panel at narrow widths. The reader never loses their place in the trace.

What it costs

45 frames is a lot of panel. Fully expanded, this stack is taller than everything else in the detail put together, and anything below it — a second span's attributes, the notifications region — is pushed off screen. The truncation shown here (31 more frames) is a patch, not a fix: it just moves the question to “how many is enough”.
