4A window of its own

Clicking the frames chip opens a dedicated window over the trace modal — the wide near-fullscreen pattern the project already uses for large content. Room for the whole stack without negotiating with anything else, plus the things a stack wants and a cramped panel cannot host: copy-as-text, an app-frames filter, a jump to the flamegraph, and per-frame open in IntelliJ through the IDE integration that already exists.

Hover an application frame to open it in IntelliJ — the profile is already linked to a running window.

What it buys

The only direction with room to be a real tool. Copy-as-text is what a reader actually does with a stack — paste it into an issue — and there is nowhere to put that button in the other four. It is also the natural home for the IDE links, which turn a stack into navigation rather than text.

What it costs

A modal on top of a modal. Two layers of dismissal, and the trace behind it is gone while you read — so the timing context that made the throw interesting is exactly what you lose. It is also the most to build: a new modal component, its own state, its own keyboard handling.
