Five formats for the notification popover

Every page draws the same two notifications in the same modal, so what differs is the panel and nothing else. Both are shown on purpose: the real one from the screenshot (PIPELINE_COMPLETED — three words and four attributes) and one worth scrolling (a paragraph and eight attributes, two past the MAX_POPOVER_ATTRIBUTES cap of 6). A format that only works at one of those extremes is caught here rather than in review.

What the audit actually found

  • Width is not the problem. 22rem ≈ 352px sits inside the band every other floating panel uses: tsc-anno-tip 280px, the flamegraph tooltip 280–400px, picker-pop 430px. What reads as cramped is max-height: 13rem, padding: 0.55rem 0.7rem, and no header band.
  • It is the only structured-detail panel in the app without a tinted header bar. .exc-dock, .sd-region, picker-pop, the flamegraph tooltip and the thread modal all have one.
  • It has no way out. .exc-dock has .dock-close; the popover has no close button, no click-away and no Escape — the only exits are clicking the same mark again or following the Select link. Every direction here adds one.
  • The two sibling panels disagree on their border. .rail-pop uses --color-border-input, .exc-dock uses --color-secondary.
  • Half of it is already consistent. .pop-attr* is byte-for-byte .sd-entry-attr*, and .pop-sev is .sd-entry-sev with a renamed variable. The popover already speaks the region's dialect — it is the shell around them that diverged.

One thing that looks like an easy win and is not

Replacing the attribute chips with Badge key-value chips looks obvious — the app uses them in 60+ places and they are visually near-identical. Do not. Badge key-value mode is not monospaced, and the region states the rule out loud: "An attribute key is the developer's own string, so it is set in mono and never relabelled." Switching would break it. None of the five does this.
