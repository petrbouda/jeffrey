What it buys

  • Full text, always — the only direction that shows every notification body without a click, at any trace size.
  • The pane is a real surface: search, severity filters, and a scope switch between the whole trace and the selected subtree.
  • Cross-highlighting works both ways — hover a line to get the cursor and the span, select a span to narrow the list.
  • Chatty traces get better here, not worse: the log scrolls, and the waterfall keeps its shape.

What it costs

  • It takes 25rem off the waterfall, which is what the modal is for. On a laptop the bars lose about a quarter of their width.
  • Below ~1080px the pane has to drop underneath, and the cross-highlight stops being visible in the same glance.
  • Two lists to keep in sync, and a hover-to-highlight relationship that no keyboard path serves for free.
  • The modal already has four modes and a layered Escape. This adds a fifth surface next to them.

What it takes to build

  • A new NotificationLogPane.vue plus a layout change in TraceSpansModal.vue: .trace-body becomes two columns.
  • Selection has to become shared state between the pane and TraceWaterfall — a hovered notification id alongside the selected span id.
  • Argues for its own endpoint, GET /{traceId}/notifications, since the pane wants paging and filtering the waterfall does not.
