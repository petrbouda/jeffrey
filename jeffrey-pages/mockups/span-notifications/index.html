Span notifications in the Trace Modal

jeffrey.Notification already exists in utilities/jeffrey-events, but it is deliberately outside tracing — the package javadoc says so out loud: "these are instants, not spans … they do not take part in traces." Attaching them to the open span is a small change (two @Contextual fields stamped from Tracer.current()); what to do with them on screen is the open question. Five directions were drawn, and the answer is a combination of two of them — the final design is first below, with the five it came from underneath it and today's behaviour as the baseline. Every page draws the same trace and the same six notifications, so what differs is layout, not data. The final page carries two further decisions: jeffrey.Alert is dropped — severity already says how serious a notification is, and a second event type can only disagree with it — and exceptions join the trace too, on a rail of their own.

Final — two rails + bar pins chosen

Directions 1 and 2 combined, because each one's main cost is the other's strength. Every instant is drawn twice on purpose: once on a rail above the span rows, which folding, filtering and critical-path-only cannot touch, and once as a pin on the bar of the span it belongs to, with a count badge on the span name and its own region in the detail panel. Clicking a rail mark opens direction 1's popover for the immediate read, and its footer is the bridge into the deep one — it selects the span underneath, which opens with the same entry highlighted. The popover opens toward the chrome rather than the bars, so what it hides is the toolbar, not the drawing.

Two families, two rails. jeffrey.Notification as diamonds coloured by severity, and jdk.JavaExceptionThrow as crosses — hollow when caught, red when they escaped — split the way the toolbar already splits Blocking ops from I/O ops. The escaped throw is matched to trace_spans.error_type, which turns today's bare class name into a class name with a stack behind it.

Row accent — five variants settled

The left edge of the two highlighted rows inside the detail panel — the CRITICAL notification and the escaped exception. Six renderings of the same two panels; option 3, the panel edge, won and is now on the final mockup: the region carries a 3px left border in the colour of the worst entry it holds, and the severe row keeps only its tint. Kept here as the record of what was weighed.

What the backend would have to provide

  • NotificationEvent gains @Contextual long traceId and long spanId, stamped at commit from Tracer.current() — 0 when no span is open, which is already the library's "no id" convention.
  • AlertEvent is removed. It is field-for-field identical to NotificationEvent, and its only claim — "this one is serious" — is what severity already carries, so the two can contradict each other (an Alert with severity=LOW). Only two call sites emit it, both already passing a Severity. The reader keeps recognising jeffrey.Alert for recordings that already contain it; what goes is the second way to write one.
  • Exceptions need no new ids and no new ingestion. jdk.JavaExceptionThrow and jdk.JavaErrorThrow are already parsed and already power the Exceptions view, landing in events with a thread_hash, a stacktrace_hash and {thrownClass, message}. A throw is always on the throwing thread, so thread_hash + innermost-containing-window is a sound attribution — unlike a notification, which can be committed off-thread and therefore has to carry the ids.
  • They must not extend AbstractTracedEvent: structural span discovery keys on a declared spanId field, so that would turn every notification into a span. This is exactly why TraceScopeEvent calls its field scopedSpanId.
  • One derived table filled beside DERIVE_TRACE_SPANS, with a kind column for the two families — typed columns, not read-time JSON extraction, for the same reason trace_spans exists. The escaped flag is a match between a throw's thrownClass and trace_spans.error_type, computed once at derivation.
  • TraceDetail carries the notifications; EVENTS_IN_SPAN must exclude the notification types the way it already excludes span, scope and promoted blocking types, or every notification is listed twice.
  • Nothing above depends on which direction wins. Only the last step — the component — does.

Two things worth deciding while you look

  • The severity palette is provisional. These mockups use danger for CRITICAL/HIGH and warning for MEDIUM — but danger is already the GC-pause band and warning is already the critical-path marker, and all three appear in the same picture. Direction 5 hedges by encoding severity in height as well as hue; the others rely on shape (a diamond is never a band).
  • Is a notification an annotation on a span, or a row in the tree? Directions 1, 2, 4 and 5 say annotation. Direction 3 says row — and pays for it: look at rows 8-10 of that page, where PAYMENT_RETRY at +158 ms is drawn after a sibling that was still running when it fired. Settled: annotation.
  • Every notification is now drawn twice. The rail mark and the pin are one notification, so they need one source of truth and one shared hover state — a picture that contradicts itself is worse than either half of it alone.
