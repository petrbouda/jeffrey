☰Row accent — five variants

Just the left edge of the two highlighted rows inside the detail panel: the CRITICAL notification and the escaped exception. Everything else on the final mockup stays as it is. Same two panels rendered six times down the page — today’s treatment first, then five alternatives, so the only difference between them is the accent itself.

A 2px inset box-shadow inside a rounded row, plus the tint. It works, but the bar is clipped by the row’s own corner radius, so it thins out at both ends — and the same device says selected elsewhere in the app, which is not what either of these rows means.

3 Panel edge chosen

The accent moves up a level: the region carries it, and the row keeps only its tint. The panel announces what it holds before you read a row, at the cost of no longer pointing at which row. This is the one on the final mockup. The edge takes the colour of the worst entry its region holds, so it is derived from the data rather than hard-coded — the same worst-of walk the row badges already do.

What is identical in all six

  • The panels are rendered from the same builders the final mockup uses, so the only thing that differs down the page is the accent CSS.
  • Both rows resolve their colour through one --accent property — the CRITICAL notification takes the severity ramp, the escaped throw takes danger — so whichever wins is written once and applies to both.
  • Everything else on the final page is unchanged.

What to look for

  • Do the two rows still read as different kinds of severe? A CRITICAL notification is the application talking; an escaped throw is the reason the span failed.
  • Does the accent still register with the row tint removed (4 and 5), where the pill and the tag carry it alone?
  • Would it survive a panel holding four severe rows rather than one? 3 stops distinguishing them; 2 starts to stripe.
