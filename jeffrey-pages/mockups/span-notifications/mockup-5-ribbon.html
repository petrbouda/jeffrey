What it buys

  • Answers the triage question first: did anything shout during this request, and roughly when — before the reader has to read a single span.
  • Severity is encoded twice, in hue and in height, so the one CRITICAL is the tallest thing in the strip whatever the palette does.
  • Callouts put the two that matter in words at trace scope; the table underneath carries the other four in full, sortable, and closed by default.
  • The waterfall itself is untouched — no new column, no new rows, no pins on the bars.

What it costs

  • Weakest span attribution of the five: the callout names the span in text, but nothing on screen draws the line to the row.
  • Callouts need placement logic and will collide — two CRITICALs 8 ms apart cannot both have a 14rem bubble.
  • Two places now list notifications (ribbon and table), and neither is where the reader is looking when they open a span.
  • A trace with 40 LOW notes and no CRITICAL gets a strip of grey ticks that says almost nothing.

What it takes to build

  • A NotificationRibbon.vue above .wf-head plus a DataTable below the waterfall — both additive, neither touching displayRows.
  • The table is the one place a shared component drops straight in: DataTable + TableToolbar + Badge, per the /data-table pattern.
  • Callout placement is the only real algorithm: rank by severity, take the top two or three, push apart on collision.
